# M0 performance results

Results of `tools/perf/measure.mjs` against the [M0 budgets](../milestones/m0.md#performance-budgets), measured as described in [testing](../design/testing.md#measuring-performance). Newest first.

## 2026-10-21, macOS: keystrokes and long replies

Same machine as the entries below, through `node tools/perf/run.mjs` and Chromium traces of `long-stream` with typing.

- **Where keystrokes wait**: `measure.mjs` now reports delivery, the time from the key event to the renderer's handler, apart. With the app idle a keystroke is delivered in 1.1 ms at p95 and reaches its frame in 13 ms, inside the budget. While `long-stream` runs, delivery took 21 of its 36 ms: keystrokes wait behind the renderer's frames, so the budget is the renderer's to meet after all, not the OS compositor's as an earlier entry concluded.
- **Why the frames grew**: every phase of a frame grew with the length of the reply, from 3.5 ms at its start to 25 ms at the end of 200 KB. Streamdown memoizes blocks, but each update still repaired and reconciled the whole text, and laid out, pre-painted and painted every one of its 1,622 blocks in one container.
- **Fix**: long replies render in chunks that end on block boundaries, each a Streamdown of its own; only the live tail re-renders. Screenshots of the whole 200 KB reply, at 21 positions and at each of its 25 chunk boundaries, are pixel-identical to an unchunked build, and so is its height. Switching to a session that holds it takes as long as before, 465 to 470 ms to first paint, most of it mounting 200 KB of markdown.

| `long-stream`, 200 KB                      | Before     | After      |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------- | ---------- |
| Frame interval, p95 / p99                  | 25 / 33 ms | 10 / 17 ms |
| Keystroke to its frame, p95                | 36 ms      | 19 ms      |
| Of which delivery, p95                     | 21 ms      | 9 to 10 ms |
| Mean frame in the last eighth of the reply | 25.4 ms    | 12.9 ms    |

- **Still growing**: React's work per frame is now flat, but style and layout, pre-paint and paint still grow with the reply, mostly compositing inputs, cull rects, intersection observers and hit tests, which walk every layer of the reply each time autoscroll moves it. Skipping settled chunks with `content-visibility: auto` removes most of it: tried without its margin handling, it took the frame p99 from 17 to 9 ms and the keystroke p95 from 19 to 17 ms, delivery from 9 to 5 ms. It is not in: its containment stops margins from collapsing between chunks, so chunks could then end only between blocks of known margins, and it helps only replies past about 16,000 characters.
- **The budget's own floor**: the keystroke is timed to the start of the frame after the one that shows it, two frame boundaries that alone take 8.3 to 16.7 ms at 120 Hz, depending on where in a frame the key lands; with the app idle it measures 13 ms at p95. Under 16 ms while a reply streams would need keystrokes delivered at once, which no rendering change gets to. Whether to time the budget to the frame that shows the letter instead is the maintainer's decision.

`.github/workflows/perf.yml` at `ccce329` ([run](https://github.com/overbench/overbench/actions/runs/39613912781)), on GitHub's `windows-latest`, `ubuntu-latest` (under Xvfb) and `macos-latest` runners. - **Launch** on runners is noise: the screenshot workflow's launches on the same runners ranged from 0.9 to 5.7 s. On Linux the first interactive frame came at 5.0 s, after the script had read the metric.
- **Windows** flushes at most one store update per frame although its timers tick about every 15 ms.
- **Linux** runs under Xvfb, where Chromium has no GPU to draw with, so its frame times compare least with the others.
- **macOS runner keystrokes**: 484 to 529 ms at p95 while its frames run at 19 to 33 ms. Delivery to the renderer's handler measured 2 ms there, so the time goes after the renderer has the key, on the runner's text input or presentation path; it does not show on hardware, where delivery is 1 ms with the app idle.
- **Later runs**, after the merged feedback fixes and [chunked replies](#2026-10-21-macos-keystrokes-and-long-replies): Linux went from 47 frames over 50 ms in `long-stream` to 7 in two runs, its frame p95 from 83 ms to 17 and 33 ms, and its keystroke p95 from 119 ms to 49 and 39 ms. Windows measured 38 and 45 frames over 50 ms, which looked like a regression, so the commits in between were measured on Windows alone: the code of the run above measured 25 this time instead of 7, the fixes without chunking 36 and 70. The Windows runner's frame intervals come in multiples of 15.6 ms, its timer tick (31, 47, 62, 78 and 94 ms), so its frame counts move in coarse steps and vary by a factor of three between runs of the same code; they cannot show a regression of this size.

## 2026-10-21, macOS: a streaming regression

Same machine and build as the entry below, through `node tools/perf/run.mjs`. Streaming had lost its smoothness since `42f218b`; `git bisect` over the 50 commits after it put it on `356c862`, which faded the thread's top edge.

| Measured                                    | `42f218b` | `166c532`, before the fix | After the fix |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------- | ------------------------- | ------------- |
| Frames over 50 ms, `long-stream`            | 1         | 25, longest 150 ms        | 1, 80 ms      |
| Frames over 50 ms, `tool-storm`             | 0         | 10                        | 0             |
| Keystroke to its frame while streaming, p95 | 35 ms     | 61 ms                     | 35 ms         |
| Frame interval, `long-stream` p95           | 25 ms     | 49 ms                     | 25 ms         |

- **Cause**: shadcn's `scroll-fade-y` masks the scroller and moves the mask with a scroll-driven animation. While the thread follows output, the timeline moves on every frame and the content in view changes under a mask that is partly transparent, and Chromium repaints the masked thread each time. Each part alone is cheap: the same top mask without the animation measured 1 frame over 50 ms, and the bottom fade alone measured 1, since it is fully opaque at the end of the thread.
- **Fix**: the top edge is now a `--background` gradient drawn over the scroller (`scroll-fade-top-overlay`), its opacity on the same scroll timeline; the bottom keeps shadcn's `scroll-fade-b`. Nothing changes on screen.
- **Scrolled up while streaming**, with the thread 11,000 px from its end, the mask on both edges, the fix and no fades at all all measured no frames over 50 ms at a p95 of 15 to 17 ms: the content in view stops changing.
- The sidebar's fade and the thread's focus ring, also from `356c862`, measured no difference.

## 2026-10-21, macOS

- **Machine**: MacBook Pro, Apple M1 Pro, 16 GB, built-in Liquid Retina XDR display at 120 Hz
- **OS**: macOS 27.0.1 (26A434)
- **App**: OverBench 0.1.0 at `42f218b` with the renderer minified, Electron 44.9.2, the unpacked production build (`electron-vite preview`)

| Budget                                                         | Result                           | Target                | Met       |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------- | --------- |
| App launch to first interactive frame, excluding agent startup | 402 to 418 ms                    | under 500 ms          | yes       |
| Renderer commits while streaming                               | at most 1 store update per frame | at most one per frame | yes       |
| Main-thread long tasks while streaming 200 updates per second  | none once warm; see below        | none over 50 ms       | see below |
| Switching to a session with 1,000 entries, to first paint      | 36 ms median, 37 ms worst of 5   | under 100 ms          | yes       |
| Keystroke to paint in the composer                             | 23 ms p95, 24 ms slowest         | under 16 ms           | no        |
| Agent host memory, excluding agent processes                   | 120 MB after `tool-storm`        | under 150 MB          | yes       |

- **Launch**: minifying the renderer took the main bundle from 2.17 MB to 0.89 MB and the launch from 416 to 441 ms to 402 to 411 ms over four launches each.
- **Long tasks**: `long-stream` (200 KB at 200 updates per second) had no long animation frame once the app had run a turn, the longest frame 33 to 50 ms. The first turn after a launch has one or two frames of 80 to 100 ms, most of it React and V8 running the rendering path for the first time; building Shiki's highlighters ahead of time in idle periods did not measurably shorten them, so it was left out. `tool-storm` (500 tool calls in about 3 s) had none in most runs, but about one run in five showed a single frame of 58 to 215 ms that a trace has not caught yet. Its frames run at p95 33 ms, mostly in MessageScroller measuring every item on each scroll event and in Base UI's Collapsible reading styles as each tool card mounts.
- **Keystroke to paint**: the main thread handles a keystroke in about 2 ms, and a trace puts it on screen within 4 to 9 ms of main-thread time. The 23 ms is measured from the key event to the start of the frame after the one that shows the letter, so it includes up to two frame intervals by construction. Event Timing, which runs to the frame's presentation, reports 16 to 48 ms while streaming; Chromium's `EventLatency` breakdown shows that about 14 ms of it is the step from the GPU swap to the display, the OS compositor, which is there with the app idle as well. As written, the budget cannot be met on macOS whatever the renderer does; how to measure it is an open question.
- **Reopening** the 1,000-entry session, which replays it with `session/load`, took 112 ms.

### What changed to get here

The first measurement of `long-stream` in a production build, on the same machine, against the run above:

| Frame interval while streaming 200 KB | Before           | After |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ----- |
| p95                                   | 50 ms            | 17 ms |
| p99                                   | 66 ms            | 25 ms |
| Longest                               | 507 ms           | 42 ms |
| Frames over 50 ms                     | 50               | 0     |
| Long animation frames                 | 46, 3.3 s in all | none  |

- Streamdown's `controls` were a new object on every render, and its context depends on them, so every chunk re-rendered every code block and table.
- Finishing a message switched Streamdown to `static` mode, which rebuilt the whole message (440 ms for 200 KB); it now stays in `streaming` mode, and the change of `isAnimating` that re-enables the code block controls goes through `useDeferredValue`.
- The growing last thread item flipped between skipped and rendered under `content-visibility: auto` on nearly every chunk, recalculating the style of up to 13,000 elements each time.
- Entry animations were timed with `duration-*`, which also sets `transition-duration`; with `transition-property: all` they transitioned the inherited `scrollbar-color` for 300 ms whenever autoscrolling stopped, recalculating the whole message on every frame.
- Switching to a session with 1,000 entries took 260 to 310 ms while every entry mounted at once; the thread now renders from its end.
